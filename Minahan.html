Minahan (Old Mining Site)

Minahan, meaning “mining area,” refers to a former small-scale mining site in the upland forests of General Nakar, Quezon. Today, it stands as a historical landmark surrounded by mountain landscapes and dense Sierra Madre vegetation, attracting trekkers and history enthusiasts.

Overview & History

The Minahan area was once used for mineral extraction activities, leaving behind traces of excavated land and remnants of mining structures. Over time, operations declined, allowing nature to reclaim much of the site. It now serves as a quiet reminder of the municipality’s resource-based past and its connection to the Sierra Madre mountain range.

Geography & Natural Features

The terrain is rugged and mountainous, with rocky soil, forest cover, small streams, and dense tropical vegetation. Cooler air is noticeable at higher elevations, and the surrounding forest ecosystem supports diverse flora and fauna typical of upland Quezon.

Tourism & Visitor Information

  • Trekking and forest exploration
  • Photography of mountain scenery
  • Educational visits about local mining history
  • Adventure hiking
  • Bring own food, water, and trekking gear
  • Registration with barangay may be required

Best visited during dry season (November–May) for safer trail conditions. Visitors must avoid unstable excavation areas and practice responsible tourism.

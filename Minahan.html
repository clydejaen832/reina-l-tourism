Minahan (Old Mining Site)

Minahan, located in the upland areas of General Nakar, Quezon, refers to a former mining site known for its historical significance and rugged natural landscape. The area features remnants of past small-scale mining activities surrounded by dense forest and mountainous terrain. Today, it attracts visitors interested in history, trekking, and off-the-beaten-path exploration.

Location & Accessibility

The Minahan site is situated within the mountainous interior of General Nakar, near rural barangays connected to the eastern slopes of the Sierra Madre. Visitors travel from the town proper to designated jump-off barangays, then proceed by trekking through forest trails and uneven terrain. Local guides are highly recommended for safe navigation.

Geography & Background

The area is characterized by rugged upland terrain, rocky surfaces, forest cover, and visible remnants of excavated land. Small streams and dense vegetation surround the site, creating a humid tropical environment with cooler air at higher elevations. The name “Minahan” reflects its historical use as a mining area, though operations have long ceased.

Tourism & Visitor Information

Minahan offers trekking, photography, and educational visits focused on local economic history and environmental awareness.

  • Trekking and nature walks
  • Exploration of historical mining remnants (where permitted)
  • Mountain and forest photography
  • Registration with barangay officials may be required
  • Bring sufficient water, food, and trekking gear
  • Dry season (November–May) is best for safer trails
